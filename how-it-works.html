You spent years as a full-time W-2 tech employee. Now, you’ve received a lucrative contract offer paying $90, $120, or $175/hour. It feels exciting, but you’re wondering: What about taxes? Health insurance? E&O? Do I need to form a Delaware LLC?

Evaluating Your Options

The 3 Ways to Handle an Independent Contract

When an employer, recruiter, or client offers you contract work, you typically face three paths:

Option 1: Traditional Agency W-2

You become a temporary employee of the staffing agency placing you.

The Reality: The agency takes a massive 25%–40% markup on your bill rate. Worse, you are on a standard W-2, meaning you cannot write off your computer, internet, or home office expenses.

Result: You leave tens of thousands of dollars on the table.

Option 2: The DIY Solo LLC (1099/C2C)

You go to the state website, register an LLC or S-Corp, open business bank accounts, and buy private insurance.

The Reality: You face 15.3% self-employment taxes, $2,000+ for commercial E&O policies, state franchise tax reports, accounting software, CPA fees, and hours spent chasing late invoices.

Result: Heavy administrative burden & compliance liability.

Option 3: IamIndependent Umbrella

Recommended

You negotiate your full top-dollar contract rate. IamIndependent acts as your Employer of Record (W-2).

The Reality: No LLC needed. Full E&O and General Liability insurance provided. You deduct business gear tax-free per paycheck. 401(k) group plan included. Timely direct deposits.

Result: Maximum net income, total freedom, zero headache.

Plain English Financials

How Pre-Tax Reimbursements Save You Real Money

Most tech workers don’t realize how taxes diminish contract income if expenses aren’t structured correctly.

Let’s Walk Through an Example

Imagine you are billing $100/hour for 40 hours a week, earning $16,000 gross per month.

As a remote tech consultant, you spend $800/month on legitimate business expenses: your home fiber internet, mobile phone plan, high-spec laptop depreciation, cloud servers for prototyping, and development software subscriptions.

Under Standard W-2 Agency Employment:

You are taxed on the entire $16,000. You pay your $800 of tech expenses out of your post-tax pocket. You cannot write this off on your tax return.

Under IamIndependent:

Your $800 is reimbursed to you pre-tax. Taxes are only calculated on the remaining $15,200. At a combined 35% tax rate, you instantly keep an extra $280 in cold, hard cash every single month — or over $3,360 per year!

Plus, you don't pay $2,500/year for individual E&O and Liability policies, and you don't pay $1,000+ to an accountant to file corporate business tax forms.
